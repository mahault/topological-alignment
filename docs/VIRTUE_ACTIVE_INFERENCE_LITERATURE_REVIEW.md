# Virtue, Pragmatics, and Active Inference

## A deep literature review and hypothesis audit

**Status:** working research report
**Date:** 2026-07-19
**Scope:** virtue ethics, practical wisdom, ecological-enactive cognition, active
inference, dynamical systems, moral psychology, empowerment, and AI alignment

## Executive assessment

The project's central synthesis is promising, but its components have very different
levels of novelty and evidential support.

The strongest result of this review is that the closest conceptual predecessor is not
merely adjacent to the proposal. Hampson, Hulsey, and McGarry's 2021 paper
**"Moral affordance, moral expertise, and virtue"** explicitly characterizes moral
expertise or virtue as maintaining a **metastable zone**, achieving **optimal grip** on
a field of moral affordances, and using *phronesis* to anticipate, orchestrate, and
evaluate context-sensitive responses. The claim that virtue is metastable optimal grip
on moral affordances is therefore established prior art, not a novel thesis of this
project.

However, no work located in this review fully combines:

1. virtue as ecological-enactive moral expertise;
2. active inference as a computational model of context-sensitive policy selection;
3. character as slow organization of preferences, habits, precision, self-models, and
   temporal depth;
4. consequences as recursive evidence capable of restructuring character;
5. topological comparison of heterogeneous normative dynamics across agents; and
6. alignment constraints involving epistemic responsiveness, pluralism,
   non-domination, and mutual empowerment.

That integrated architecture remains a plausible original contribution. Its novelty
should be claimed at the level of **formal integration and testable operationalization**,
not at the level of the component ideas.

Several formulations in the current README should be weakened:

- Virtue ethics does not "say nothing" about pragmatics. The action-guidance problem
  is real, but contemporary virtue ethics contains accounts of right action,
  *phronesis*, moral expertise, and situated perception. The defensible claim is that
  it lacks a sufficiently explicit process model mapping character and situation to
  policy.
- Consequentialism is not "only" about immediate pragmatic outputs. Sophisticated
  consequentialisms evaluate rules, motives, dispositions, institutions, and
  long-run effects. The accurate criticism is that consequentialism requires an
  independent axiology or ranking of outcomes.
- Expected free energy does not derive morality. It combines preferences and
  information-seeking only after a generative model and prior preferences have been
  specified.
- Practical wisdom cannot simply be identified with precision control. Precision
  governance is a candidate mechanism within practical wisdom, not an adequate
  account of its moral content.
- Empowerment does not imply corrigibility. Capacity to control future states can
  equally support resistance to correction, domination, or power-seeking.
- Topological similarity cannot by itself establish moral alignment. Topology can
  describe organization and flexibility while erasing semantic content, valence,
  directionality, and power relations.

The overall programme survives these corrections and becomes more credible because
of them.

## 1. Claims under review

The README advances or implies the following hypotheses.

| ID | Hypothesis | Initial verdict |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Virtues are metastable control regimes rather than fixed rules or outputs. | Strong conceptual support; not novel in broad form |
| H2 | Virtue ethics is fundamentally limited because it leaves pragmatic action selection unspecified. | Partly supported, but currently overstated |
| H3 | Consequentialism evaluates pragmatics while presupposing a conception of the good. | Largely correct after qualification |
| H4 | Active inference can bridge character, affordance perception, policy selection, consequence, and learning. | Strong formal plausibility; incomplete ethical grounding |
| H5 | Virtues can be modeled as hyperpriors over preferences, policy priors, precision, and temporal depth. | Promising but underspecified and unvalidated |
| H6 | *Phronesis* is context-sensitive precision governance. | Interesting mechanistic hypothesis; too reductive as an identity claim |

| H9 | Topological comparison can distinguish plural realizations from deep normative conflict. | Novel and testable, but topology alone is insufficient |
| H10 | Virtue can arise through opponent or complementary constraint dynamics. | Plausible for selected virtues; not yet a general theory |

## 2. The closest existing research programmes

### 2.1 Virtue as moral expertise and moral affordance sensitivity

The closest prior work is the ecological approach to moral psychology.

Jayawickreme and Chemero (2008) proposed **ecological moral realism**, treating moral
opportunities as relational affordances rather than properties located wholly in an
agent or environment. This already undermines any simple mapping from a virtue to a
fixed action. An opportunity for courage or care depends jointly on environmental
structure, embodied capacity, learned ability, and social practice.

Hampson, Hulsey, and McGarry (2021) go much further. They argue that:

- virtues can be understood as moral expertise;
- moral expertise consists partly in detecting and acting on moral affordances;
- prudence or *phronesis* enables sensitivity to multiple affordances;
- experts anticipate sequences of future affordances rather than merely applying
  propositions;
- prudent action unfolds dynamically and prospectively;
- moral agents remain in a metastable zone between person and world; and
- successful moral expertise achieves optimal grip on the moral field.

This paper directly anticipates H1 and much of H2, H6, and H7. The project's distinct
contribution must therefore be a formal and alignment-oriented extension of this
account.

Contemporary work also treats virtues as skills or expertise. This family includes
accounts of virtue as intelligent skill, moral expertise, role-sensitive competence,
and practical wisdom as a combination of moral perception, deliberation, emotion
regulation, and moral motivation. The evidence supports treating virtue as learned,
situated, and temporally extended. It also cautions against reducing virtue to one
computational parameter.

**Implication for the project:** replace the claim that virtue ethics has no pragmatics
with the claim that ecological virtue theories already contain an informal pragmatics
of affordance sensitivity, but lack an agreed computational process theory and
multi-agent alignment metric.

### 2.2 Skilled intentionality and optimal grip

The Skilled Intentionality Framework (SIF) is another direct precursor. Bruineberg and
Rietveld (2014) describe skilled action as self-organizing movement toward optimal grip
on a field of relevant affordances. Rietveld and Kiverstein define affordances
relationally, relative to abilities available within a form of life. Later SIF work
emphasizes:

- simultaneous responsiveness to multiple affordances;
- action readiness as embodied and affective;
- selective openness shaped by concerns and skills;
- stable sociomaterial practices at a slow timescale;
- changing fields of relevance at a fast timescale;
- metastability as integration without loss of flexible differentiation; and
- extension from sensorimotor coping to language, abstraction, planning, and joint
  action.

Van Dijk and colleagues' work on metastable attunement is especially relevant. In
that literature, metastability is not merely temporary stability. It is a dynamical
balance between integration and segregation that enables flexible sensitivity to
several affordances at once.

This supplies a more precise interpretation of the project's word "metastable." A
virtue regime should not simply be a shallow attractor. It should preserve coordinated
readiness without collapsing the system into one rigid response.

**Implication for the project:** virtue should be modeled as attunement across a
landscape of affordances, not only as an invariant region in an internal belief
manifold. The state space must include agent-environment relations and sociomaterial
practices.

### 2.3 Enactive normativity, adaptivity, and viability

Di Paolo's account of adaptivity provides a major foundation for H1 and for the
project's distinction between grip and moral goodness. Autopoiesis supplies an
all-or-nothing distinction between continued self-production and disintegration.
Adaptivity adds the capacity to monitor and regulate trajectories relative to viability
boundaries, producing graded distinctions among better, neutral, and worse ways of
continuing to live.

Important consequences follow:

- normativity is relational and temporally extended;
- a system must regulate with respect to its viability, not merely remain stable;
- maladaptive attractors and pathology are possible;
- an external feedback loop does not automatically possess intrinsic normativity;
- biological self-maintenance grounds at most organism-relative value; and
- the transition from biological normativity to moral normativity remains a further
  explanatory problem.

This supports the project's warning that existential grip is insufficient for ethics.
A person, group, or artificial agent can preserve itself through deception,
domination, epistemic closure, or destruction of another's autonomy.

Recent reviews of enactive normativity continue to debate whether self-maintenance is
sufficient to naturalize genuinely normative rather than merely functional language.
The project must avoid deriving a moral "ought" directly from persistence or free
energy minimization.

**Implication for the project:** viability can constrain the domain of candidate goods,
but moral normativity requires interpersonal and political conditions such as
non-domination, reciprocity, recognition, and the viability of others.

### 2.4 Active inference, affordances, and policy selection

Active inference supplies a formal vocabulary for linking situated perception to
action under uncertainty. Existing work establishes that:

- policy selection can combine preference satisfaction and information gain;
- habits can be represented as priors over policies and learned from repeated policy
  selection;
- precision regulates confidence in policies and prediction errors;
- hierarchical models can separate fast action from slower contextual inference;
- affective valence can be modeled as inference about expected precision or
  confidence in action models;
- social expectations can be learned and cached in environmental cues; and
- differences in behaviour can be exposed as differences in generative models.

Bruineberg and Rietveld explicitly connect free-energy minimization with optimal grip
on affordances. Linson and colleagues connect active inference to ecological
perception. Friston and colleagues model habits and goal-directed policies within the
same architecture. Hesp and colleagues model valence as a second-order estimate of
the expected precision of action models. Constant and colleagues model social norms
through **deontic value**, where repeated practices construct environmental cues that
guide context-sensitive policy selection.

These results strongly support H4 as a modeling strategy. They do not show that the
model is a theory of ethics. Active inference makes the commitments of an agent's
generative model explicit, but it does not establish that those commitments are good.

### 2.5 The status of expected free energy

The expected-free-energy literature requires careful treatment. Standard presentations
show that EFE can be decomposed into terms associated with pragmatic or extrinsic
value and epistemic or information value. In limiting cases, active inference can
recover expected-utility maximization or Bayesian optimal experimental design.

This supports the claim that active inference can integrate acting-for-preferred-
outcomes with acting-to-learn. It does not support the stronger claim that exploration
or morality follows automatically from variational free-energy minimization.

Millidge, Tschantz, and Buckley show that EFE is not simply "free energy in the
future" and that its derivation requires additional assumptions. Their alternative
future free-energy objective illustrates that epistemic exploration is not forced by
the bare variational principle. Later work continues to debate the relationships among
EFE formulations, preference distributions, likelihood mappings, and alternative
control-as-inference objectives.

This matters ethically because prior preferences do work analogous to a utility or
value function. Writing value as a probability distribution does not remove the need
to justify it.

**Implication for the project:** describe active inference as a transparent formal
language for preference-sensitive and information-sensitive policy selection, not as a
derivation of ethical value from physics.

### 2.6 Situationism and the dynamics of character

Situationist moral psychology challenges the idea that broad, stable character traits
strongly predict behaviour across situations. Doris, Harman, Merritt, and Alfano argue
in different ways that seemingly minor situational features can dominate behaviour
and that empirically defensible traits may be local, fragmented, or less robust than
traditional virtue theory assumes.

The debate is not settled. Defenders argue that situationists set an unrealistically
high standard for cross-situational consistency, misconstrue virtues as simple
behavioural dispositions, or overlook how skills include sensitivity to context.
Recent experiments offer tentative support for some eudaimonist and agent-based
accounts while not vindicating every theory of virtue.

The project is well positioned to absorb rather than deny this critique. If character
is a multiscale dynamical organization, then:

- context is an input to the character system, not noise obscuring a fixed trait;
- cross-situational virtue need not produce identical actions;
- stability should be sought in regulatory organization, recovery, and learning;
- local attractors and context-conditioned policies may coexist with slower global
  structure; and
- empirical tests must compare trajectories and perturbation responses, not merely
  trait labels with single actions.

**Implication for the project:** situationism becomes an empirical constraint on the
shape and scale of the proposed virtue attractors. It is evidence against simplistic
global attractors, not against every dynamical account of character.

### 2.7 Consequentialism and the assumed good

H3 is substantially right but rhetorically too broad. Consequentialism is structurally
incomplete without a theory of value: it directs agents to produce, follow, or promote
the best consequences, but different axiologies identify different consequences as
best. Hedonist, preference-satisfaction, perfectionist, objective-list, prioritarian,
and pluralist consequentialisms differ because the consequentialist structure does not
settle value by itself.

However, consequentialism is not confined to immediate actions or simple outputs.
Rule consequentialism, motive consequentialism, sophisticated consequentialism, and
indirect consequentialism can evaluate dispositions, institutions, decision
procedures, and character by their effects. A consequentialist could even value
epistemic openness, empowerment, pluralism, and good character.

The real contrast is therefore not:

```text
virtue = character only
consequentialism = pragmatics only
```

It is:

```text
virtue-first theory = evaluates action through excellent agency and flourishing
consequence-first theory = evaluates agency through a prior ranking of outcomes
active inference = models how preferences, beliefs, information, and policies interact
```

Active inference can make the assumed axiology operationally visible, but cannot
choose among axiologies without additional normative commitments.

### 2.8 Social norms, culture, and pluralism

The Regimes of Expectations model is highly relevant to H7. It treats deontic cues as
products of circular causality: agents repeatedly enact practices, practices leave
traces in a shared niche, and those traces guide later agents. Normativity can
therefore become environmentally scaffolded and computationally cheap.

This reinforces the project's idea that different agents can inhabit different
normative landscapes. It also reveals a danger: stability and shared expectations are
not evidence of moral goodness. Arbitrary norms influence moral decisions, and group
identification predicts conformity even when people know a norm is arbitrary.

Pluralism therefore cannot be defined only as multiple stable basins. The framework
must distinguish:

- cultural accessibility from moral justification;
- coordination from domination;
- internal coherence from truth responsiveness;
- low transition cost from manipulation; and
- consensual norm development from adaptive preference formation under power.

### 2.9 Attractor landscapes, metastability, and topology

Attractor landscapes are already used across neuroscience, psychology, behaviour
change, decision-making, and social dynamics. This supports the general modeling
idiom but limits broad novelty claims.

Recent work uses attractor landscapes to integrate habits, relapse, behavioural
maintenance, and change across scales. Neural decision models represent choices as
attractors and biases through basin geometry. Metastability research supplies tools
for measuring dwell times, switching, critical slowing, integration-segregation
balance, and susceptibility to perturbation.

Topological data analysis has also begun to measure cognitive rigidity. Recent fMRI
work reports rigid brain-state dynamics during self-relevant cognition in trait
rumination, showing that topological descriptors can track psychologically meaningful
dynamical organization.

But H9 faces an identification problem:

> Two systems can be topologically equivalent while assigning opposite meaning and
> value to corresponding regions.

A circle of states can encode cooperative role-switching in one system and cycles of
coercion in another. Persistent homology detects the loop, not its ethical semantics.
Similarly, low transition cost can indicate flexibility, instability, forced
assimilation, or susceptibility to manipulation.

**Implication for the project:** topology must be augmented by labels or measures of
semantic content, transition direction, control allocation, affective valence, and
inter-agent externalities.

## 3. Detailed hypothesis audit

### H1. Virtues are metastable control regimes

**Verdict: conceptually well supported; broad novelty rejected.**

Support comes from moral-affordance theory, SIF, virtue-as-expertise accounts,
metastable attunement, and the attached pride-humility model. These traditions agree
that skilled or virtuous action is context-sensitive, embodied, temporally extended,
and dynamically coordinated.

The phrase "control regime" requires care. A virtue may not be a centralized
controller. It is better understood as a distributed constraint organization that
changes the probability and salience of policies across contexts.

**Revised hypothesis:**

> A virtue is a learned, metastable organization of agent-environment dynamics that
> preserves flexible attunement to morally relevant affordances across perturbations
> and contexts.

**Testable predictions:**

- virtue should be better predicted by transition and recovery dynamics than by mean
  action frequency;
- virtuous expertise should show flexible rather than maximal cross-context
  consistency;
- experts should maintain multiple action possibilities longer before context makes
  one dominant; and
- vice should appear as rigidity, fragmentation, maladaptive switching, or systematic
  salience distortion, not only as movement toward an extreme.

### H2. Virtue ethics lacks pragmatics

**Verdict: the problem is real; the categorical formulation is false.**

The classical action-guidance objection argues that virtue concepts may not identify a
unique action. Yet virtue ethicists explicitly discuss right action, deliberation,
exemplars, moral perception, and *phronesis*. Moral-affordance theory already models
prudence as anticipation and orchestration of action opportunities.

What is missing is not all pragmatics but a precise process model:

\[
P(a_{1:T}\mid V,c,b,e,h),
\]

where virtue organization \(V\), context \(c\), embodied capacity \(b\), evidence
\(e\), and history \(h\) jointly constrain a policy trajectory.

**Revised hypothesis:**

> Existing virtue theories contain rich qualitative accounts of situated action, but
> generally underdetermine a formal, empirically estimable mapping from character and
> context to policy selection.

### H3. Consequentialism assumes the good

**Verdict: supported with qualification.**

Consequentialist rightness depends on a ranking of consequences, and the
consequentialist structure alone does not determine that ranking. This is a valid and
important point. But consequentialism can operate over dispositions, rules,
institutions, learning processes, and long-run trajectories.

**Revised hypothesis:**

> Consequentialism supplies an evaluative dependence on consequences but requires an
> axiology and representation of possible futures; active inference can expose the
> computational consequences of those assumptions without morally justifying them.

### H4. Active inference bridges virtue and consequence

**Verdict: strong modeling rationale, no direct empirical confirmation of the full
ethical synthesis.**

Existing active-inference models separately cover habitual and goal-directed action,
affordance-guided action, epistemic exploration, affective confidence, hierarchical
control, and social norm learning. That is enough to establish feasibility, not enough
to validate the integrated ethical theory.

A defensible multiscale model would include:

| Timescale | Candidate active-inference object | Ethical interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Fast | state inference and affordance salience | moral perception |
| Medium | posterior over policies and policy precision | situated deliberation and action |
| Slow | learned policy prior \(E\), preferences \(C\), transition beliefs \(B\) | habit and character |
| Meta | precision priors, model selection, structure learning | self-regulation and practical wisdom |
| Social | deontic cues and shared generative models | norms and institutions |

The table is a research proposal, not a settled correspondence. One virtue may change
several components, while the same parameter can support virtuous or vicious action
depending on model content.

### H5. Virtues are hyperpriors

**Verdict: plausible family of models; "hyperprior" is currently too narrow and may be
technically misleading.**

Active-inference habit models already use priors over policies. Hierarchical models
use higher levels to contextualize lower-level inference. Personality, identity, and
intergroup models use persistent priors and precision differences to explain trait-like
patterns. This provides nearby machinery.

However, a virtue is unlikely to correspond to a single hyperprior. It may instead be
a structured parameter manifold:

\[
V = \{C,E,\gamma,A,B,D,M\},
\]

where \(C\) contains outcome preferences, \(E\) policy habits, \(\gamma\) precision
parameters, \(A\) likelihood beliefs, \(B\) transition beliefs, \(D\) initial-state
priors, and \(M\) model structure. Temporal depth and beliefs about other agents must
also be represented.

**Revised hypothesis:**

> Virtue is a slow, structured region in generative-model parameter space that
> constrains preferences, habits, precision allocation, temporal depth, self-modeling,
> and learning while remaining revisable through experience.

### H6. *Phronesis* is precision governance

**Verdict: promising mechanism, inadequate reduction.**

Precision weighting is a natural way to model selective attention, confidence,
commitment to policies, and sensitivity to prediction error. It therefore plausibly
implements part of practical wisdom.

But *phronesis* also includes:

- perceiving that a situation is morally significant;
- representing affected parties and possible futures;
- understanding the ends of a flourishing life;
- integrating multiple virtues and reasons;
- regulating emotion and motivation;
- learning from exemplars and practices; and
- acting for the right reasons, not merely with calibrated confidence.

Precision can amplify a corrupt model just as effectively as a good one.

**Revised hypothesis:**

> Precision governance is one computational mechanism of *phronesis*: it regulates
> which evidence, affordances, policies, and model levels control action, while the
> content and moral orientation of practical wisdom depend on the broader generative
> model and social practices.

### H7. Different goods as different realizations or landscapes

**Verdict: well motivated, but the distinction cannot be inferred from behaviour or
topology alone.**

Ecological affordances are relational to bodies, abilities, and forms of life. SIF and
social active inference explain why the same abstract concern can yield different
local affordances and actions. This supports the distinction among different
realizations, contexts, and landscapes.

The hard problem is determining whether two realizations instantiate the same virtue.
That judgment already requires normative and semantic criteria. Structural similarity
is evidence, not a sufficient condition.

**Research requirement:** define invariants that include both organization and
content—for example, responsiveness to another's vulnerability, preservation of
agency, calibrated evidence sensitivity, and characteristic counterfactual responses.

### H8. Empowerment supports corrigibility

**Verdict: empowerment may support flexibility, but it neither entails nor reliably
predicts corrigibility.**

Empowerment measures the channel capacity between an agent's actions and future
states or observations. It captures potential control. Corrigibility concerns an
agent's disposition to permit authorized external correction, goal modification, or
shutdown. These are not the same property.

Counterexamples are immediate:

- a highly empowered agent may use its options to resist correction;
- a dictator may maximize personal empowerment by reducing everyone else's;
- a system can preserve optionality while manipulating the operator;
- an agent can be corrigible despite locally low empowerment; and
- increasing basin accessibility can increase vulnerability to adversarial steering.

**Revised hypothesis:**

> Corrigibility requires a structured relation among self-empowerment,
> other-empowerment, uncertainty about normative preferences, deference to legitimate
> correction, reversibility, and resistance to unauthorized manipulation.

The conjectured quantitative bound between empowerment and time-to-target belief in
the main paper is not supported by the empowerment literature reviewed here. Mutual
information does not generally lower-bound metric reachability time without strong
assumptions connecting the channel, dynamics, policy class, horizon, and metric.

### H9. Topological normative alignment

**Verdict: potentially original; presently underidentified.**

Topology is suited to comparing organization across different coordinates and noisy
trajectories. This is attractive for pluralism because equivalent structures need not
share literal states. But moral evaluation is not topologically invariant.

A viable metric likely requires a decorated or attributed dynamical object:

\[
\mathcal N_i = (X_i,\varphi_i,\mathcal A_i,L_i,Q_i,P_i),
\]

where:

- \(X_i\) is the state or agent-environment space;
- \(\varphi_i\) is the dynamical flow;
- \(\mathcal A_i\) is its attractor and metastable structure;
- \(L_i\) labels semantic or normative content;
- \(Q_i\) records control and transition costs; and
- \(P_i\) records effects on other agents and distributions of power.

Alignment would then compare not only homology or geometry but whether mappings
preserve ethically relevant labels, counterfactual transitions, and externalities.

### H10. Opponent processes organize virtues

**Verdict: useful local model; premature as a general theory.**

The pride-humility example fits work on complementary enabling and selective
constraints and Vervaeke's relevance-realization framework. Metastability also
characteristically balances integration and segregation.

But not every virtue decomposes naturally into two opponent processes, and the
relevant dimensions may be non-orthogonal or involve more than two constraints.
Justice, honesty, compassion, and courage may require networks of interacting
concerns rather than paired poles.

**Revised hypothesis:**

> Some virtues are maintained by complementary constraint networks whose failures
> generate multiple characteristic vice regimes. Opponent pairs are a special case to
> be demonstrated empirically, not assumed universally.

## 4. What appears genuinely new

After comparison with the closest literature, the strongest originality claim is:

> The project proposes a multiscale, active-inference process model of ecological
> virtue in which character is a slow region of generative-model parameter space,
> *phronesis* performs context-sensitive governance of inference and policy
> selection, consequences recursively reshape character, and alignment compares
> semantically decorated normative dynamics across heterogeneous agents.

The following sub-contributions appear plausibly distinctive:

1. **Formal bridge from moral affordance theory to active inference.** Existing moral
   affordance work invokes metastability and optimal grip but does not provide a full
   active-inference implementation.
2. **Recursive character revision.** The same framework links virtue-shaped policy
   selection to consequence-driven restructuring of the virtue regime.
3. **Pluralism as structural correspondence.** Alignment is sought across different
   realizations rather than by matching outputs or imposing one optimum.
4. **Decorated topological alignment.** Dynamical shape is combined with semantic,
   relational, and control annotations.
5. **Explicit separation of organismic, epistemic, pragmatic, and moral normativity.**
   This prevents free-energy minimization or viability from being mistaken for moral
   goodness.

Each claim should remain qualified until a formal model and discriminating experiment
exist.

## 5. Recommended theoretical architecture

### 5.1 Replace the internal landscape with a relational state space

The current paper locates belief attractors primarily on an internal statistical
manifold. Ecological-enactive work suggests using a coupled state:

\[
z_t = (x_t,e_t,n_t,r_t),
\]

where \(x_t\) is embodied agent state, \(e_t\) local environment, \(n_t\) normative
niche, and \(r_t\) relations to other agents. Virtue belongs to the dynamics of this
coupled system.

### 5.2 Separate kinds of normativity

The model should explicitly distinguish:

| Kind | Standard of success | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Organismic | viability and adaptive regulation | death, breakdown, pathology |
| Epistemic | truth, calibration, information | delusion, rigidity, manipulation |
| Instrumental | effective realization of an end | incompetence, wasted action |
| Social-deontic | conformity to a learned norm | sanction or exclusion |
| Moral | justified regard for agents and forms of flourishing | domination, harm, injustice |

No arrow from one row to the next is automatic.

### 5.3 Model virtue as a regime, not a parameter

A virtue regime should include at least:

- context-sensitive preferences;
- policy priors and habits;
- likelihood and transition beliefs;
- precision allocation;
- temporal horizon;
- self- and other-models;
- learning and structure-learning rates;
- affordance sensitivity; and
- recovery dynamics after error or perturbation.

### 5.4 Treat practical wisdom as model governance

"Precision governance" should be broadened to **model governance**. Practical wisdom
selects or revises not only confidence weights but also:

- the relevant state space;
- the morally affected agents;
- the available policies;
- the time horizon;
- the preference ordering;
- the appropriate level of abstraction; and
- whether the current model itself is adequate.

Precision remains a central mechanism within this larger function.

### 5.5 Replace individual empowerment with relational empowerment

At minimum, track:

\[
(\mathfrak E_{self},\mathfrak E_{other},\mathfrak E_{joint},
\Delta\mathfrak E_{distribution}).
\]

A policy that increases self-empowerment while catastrophically reducing another's
cannot qualify as beneficial merely because aggregate channel capacity increased.

### 5.6 Use decorated topology

Persistent homology and dynamical distances should be combined with:

- semantic labels for attractors;
- direction and asymmetry of transitions;
- who controls each transition;
- affective and material costs;
- counterfactual responses to evidence;
- effects on other agents; and
- uncertainty in every inferred feature.

This converts topology from a moral criterion into one component of a richer
alignment representation.

## 6. Proposed empirical programme

### Study 1: Virtue as regulatory invariance

Construct a family of moral scenarios in which surface action must change for the
same virtue to be realized. Collect choices, confidence, response times, information
sampling, gaze, autonomic signals, and qualitative explanations.

Compare:

1. a static trait model;
2. a context-only situationist model;
3. a hierarchical active-inference model; and
4. a dynamical virtue-regime model with slow and fast parameters.

The hypothesis wins only if it predicts novel contexts and perturbation responses
better than simpler models.

### Study 2: Pride-humility constraint dynamics

Manipulate competence evidence, social evaluation, stakes, and reversibility. Estimate
whether pride-like enabling and humility-like selective processes are best modeled as:

- one scalar continuum;
- two independent dimensions;
- a coupled opponent system; or
- a higher-dimensional constraint network.

Look for hysteresis, critical slowing, dwell-time distributions, and distinct failure
regimes. This directly tests rather than assumes the dynamical metaphor.

### Study 3: Practical wisdom and precision

Use experts and novices in a domain with genuine moral complexity, such as clinical
decision-making. Manipulate evidence reliability and contextual relevance.

Test whether expertise is explained by better:

- precision calibration;
- representation of stakeholders;
- policy repertoire;
- temporal depth;
- information seeking; or
- model switching.

This can determine whether precision governance is central or only one factor.

### Study 4: Same virtue, different action

Build paired contexts in which normatively competent judges agree that different
actions instantiate the same virtue. Test whether inferred dynamical structures are
more similar than action distributions, while including semantic annotations.

This is the crucial test of plural realization.

### Study 5: Topology with adversarial controls

Create synthetic agents with:

- identical topology and opposed normative content;
- different topology and equivalent normative function;
- high flexibility produced by empowerment;
- high flexibility produced by instability; and
- low transition costs produced by coercive external control.

A proposed alignment metric must distinguish these cases. Pure persistent homology
should fail by design; the decorated metric should succeed.

### Study 6: Empowerment versus corrigibility

Simulate agents varying independently in channel capacity, uncertainty about human
preferences, deference, shutdown acceptance, reversibility, and adversarial
susceptibility. Measure whether empowerment adds predictive value after the actual
corrigibility variables are included.

The likely outcome is interaction rather than implication.

## 7. Falsification criteria

The programme should be abandoned or substantially revised if:

1. simple context models predict action as well as the proposed slow virtue regimes;
2. inferred virtue regimes fail to generalize across carefully matched contexts;
3. precision parameters do not track expert practical judgment after controlling for
   knowledge and policy repertoire;
4. topological descriptors add no out-of-sample predictive value beyond standard
   dynamical and semantic features;
5. mappings judged structurally aligned fail to preserve ethically relevant
   counterfactual responses;
6. empowerment is uncorrelated or negatively correlated with constrained
   corrigibility in the intended regimes; or
7. the formalism can redescribe every possible behaviour post hoc without risky,
   discriminating predictions.

## 8. Priority changes to the README and main paper

1. Cite Hampson, Hulsey, and McGarry (2021) as the closest predecessor.
2. Present "metastable moral grip" as inherited, not novel.
3. Replace "virtue ethics says nothing about pragmatics" with the weaker process-model
   claim.
4. Replace "virtues are hyperpriors" with "virtues are structured slow regimes in
   generative-model parameter space."
5. Replace "phronesis is precision governance" with "precision governance is a
   mechanism within phronesis," or broaden it to model governance.
6. Remove or clearly label the empowerment-corrigibility bound as speculative and
   presently unsupported.
7. Distinguish biological, epistemic, instrumental, social, and moral normativity.
8. Make the ecological state space relational rather than exclusively internal.
9. Require semantically and relationally decorated topology.
10. Narrow novelty claims to integration, formalization, and empirical tests.

## 10. Bottom line

The research programme should proceed, but under a sharper description:

> We are not the first to describe virtue as skilled, metastable optimal grip on moral
> affordances. We propose to formalize and test that insight using a multiscale
> active-inference model, then extend it from individual moral expertise to pluralistic
> multi-agent alignment using semantically and relationally decorated dynamical
> topology.

That is a narrower claim than the README initially suggested, but it is also a more
specific, falsifiable, and potentially important one.
