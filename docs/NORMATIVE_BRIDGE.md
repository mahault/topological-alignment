# From Phenotype-Relative Good to Moral Goodness

## Status and purpose

This document states the project's normative bridge. It is a philosophical argument,
not a theorem derived from active inference, viability theory, evolution, topology,
or the free-energy principle. The formal results establish conditional facts about
what is good *for* a declared phenotype. They deliberately do not establish whose
good matters morally or what may permissibly be done when goods conflict.

The proposed bridge is a **vulnerability-and-justifiability view**:

> A system has prima facie moral standing when there is credible evidence that it is
> a temporally integrated centre of valenced or practically organized vulnerability:
> conditions can genuinely go better or worse for it, those differences are not
> exhausted by an observer's project, and it can be benefited, harmed, enabled, or
> dominated. Exercises of power affecting such centres are morally admissible only
> when they protect a defensible floor of agency and flourishing, avoid arbitrary
> domination, remain responsive to evidence and affected perspectives, and can be
> justified to those affected under conditions that preserve voice and contestation.

This proposal is pluralist and threshold-constrained. It does not equate morality
with maximizing aggregate viability, preference satisfaction, empowerment, or
expected free energy.

## 1. The bridge argument

The argument has six premises. Each premise is exposed because rejecting one should
identify exactly where disagreement lies.

### P1 -- Real prudential difference

Some organized systems are such that conditions can non-derivatively go better or
worse *for them*. Their organization sustains needs, vulnerabilities, capacities,
projects, experiences, or forms of participation across time.

This excludes merely instrumental objects whose apparent “good” is wholly assigned
by an external user. A thermostat has a set point, but a set point alone does not show
that anything matters to the thermostat.

### P2 -- Reasons against avoidable damage

When an entity has a real good, facts about seriously damaging that good supply at
least a prima facie reason against doing so. Without this premise, even extreme,
avoidable suffering or destruction would have no normative relevance merely because
it occurred in another centre.

“Prima facie” matters: the reason can conflict with other reasons and does not imply
that every preference or current adaptation must be satisfied.

### P3 -- Non-arbitrary symmetry

If feature set (F) makes my vulnerability morally reason-giving, relevantly similar
instances of (F) in others must also count unless a further morally relevant
difference is supplied. Mere identity, group membership, ownership, proximity, or
power is not such a difference by itself.

This yields equal *considerability*, not necessarily identical treatment. Different
needs and capacities can require different responses.

### P4 -- Relational power changes the moral object

Morality evaluates not only endpoint welfare but the relations through which outcomes
are produced. Being subject to uncontrolled, discretionary power is a distinct
vulnerability even when the powerful party happens to choose benevolently. Voice,
exit, veto, information, agenda setting, and avenues of appeal therefore matter in
addition to experienced or predicted welfare.

### P5 -- Justification must survive affected perspectives

An exercise of power is presumptively defective when it depends on excluding the
evidence, claims, or contestation of those who bear its risks. Justifiability is not
actual unanimous consent: coercion, misinformation, adaptive preference, incapacity,
and conflicts of interest can corrupt consent. It requires reasons that affected
parties could assess under conditions supporting information, voice, and freedom from
retaliation.

### P6 -- Moral judgment is corrigible

Neither current preferences nor inherited virtue concepts are infallible. A morally
admissible regime must preserve ways to discover error, hear excluded standpoints,
revise its model of goods, repair damage, and alter the distribution of control.

### Conclusion

Together these premises support a constrained conclusion:

> A regime is morally eligible, rather than merely functionally successful, only if
> it treats the robust goods of all credibly affected centres as reasons; protects
> defensible capability floors; avoids arbitrary domination and hidden
> externalization; and remains publicly justifiable, contestable, epistemically
> responsive, and repairable.

This establishes an admissibility test, not a complete ranking of every eligible
regime. Moral pluralism can leave multiple permissible and incomparable outcomes.

## 2. Who or what has standing?

Standing should be assigned by evidence, not by stipulating that every dynamically
stable system has a good. The following indicators are defeasible and need not all be
present:

1. valenced experience or credible markers of sentience;
2. self-maintaining vulnerability with endogenous error correction;
3. temporally integrated interests, projects, or practical identity;
4. learning that reorganizes behaviour around benefit and damage;
5. agency, communication, refusal, or participation in shared practices;
6. dependence relations through which the entity can be enabled or dominated; and
7. a distinction between the entity's own organization and an externally imposed
   objective.

### Standing under uncertainty

Uncertainty should not be converted into a binary declaration of no standing. Use a
precautionary policy when:

- the possible harm is severe or irreversible;
- evidence of standing is credible but incomplete;
- protective measures are proportionate; and
- interested designers or owners control the evidence.

Uncertainty may affect confidence and precaution without implying that moral worth is
a simple numerical probability.

### Artificial systems

Optimization, fluent self-report, or a reward signal is insufficient by itself. An
artificial system would require evidence that there is a system-level subject of
benefit and harm rather than a simulation optimized for an operator. Until then, its
functional phenotype can be modeled without assuming moral patienthood. Its effects
on humans, animals, institutions, and ecosystems remain morally assessable regardless.

### Collectives and ecosystems

Collectives can have functional goods and may warrant derivative or independent
protection, but the framework must not let “system viability” erase the standing of
members. Claims of collective standing therefore carry an anti-sacrifice constraint:
preserving the whole does not automatically license treating members as replaceable
components.

## 3. Capability floors

A capability floor is a hard or strongly protected threshold below which gains
elsewhere do not ordinarily compensate. The initial categories are:

1. bodily and material security;
2. freedom from severe suffering and degrading treatment;
3. access to necessities required by the phenotype;
4. meaningful voice and access to relevant information;
5. exit, refusal, or appeal where relationships expose one to discretionary power;
6. practical agency and a non-trivial option set;
7. social recognition and protection against status subordination;
8. epistemic participation, including the ability to challenge how one's interests
   are represented; and
9. prospects of repair when harm occurs.

The list contains three layers that must not be conflated:

- **universal protections:** constraints against severe suffering, destruction,
  coercion, and degrading domination;
- **phenotype-relative requirements:** what a particular kind of embodied or social
  agent needs to function and flourish; and
- **institutionally specified guarantees:** thresholds selected through legitimate,
  revisable public procedures.

Floors are not automatically absolute. Emergency exceptions require necessity,
proportionality, least-harm means, equal consideration, time limits, independent
review, documentation, and duties of repair. Repeated “exceptions” indicate that the
institution is externalizing its ordinary operation onto vulnerable parties.

## 4. Non-domination and relational empowerment

An agent is dominated when another possesses uncontrolled capacity to interfere with
its basic choices or conditions, whether or not interference occurs on every occasion.
This is why favourable outcomes and high total empowerment are insufficient.

Relational assessment should separately represent:

- the focal agent's meaningful options;
- affected others' meaningful options;
- genuinely joint capabilities that require cooperation;
- control over agendas and model definitions;
- voice, veto, exit, appeal, and repair;
- dependency and exposure to retaliation; and
- the distribution, not merely the sum, of control.

Joint capability is not present when one party can unilaterally define the goal,
withdraw necessities, or replace the other's contribution. A cooperative outcome can
increase total capability while remaining dominating if control is concentrated.

## 5. Conflict procedure without a single utility function

When genuine goods conflict, use the following ordered procedure:

1. **Describe all affected centres and uncertainties.** Include indirect, future,
   structurally excluded, and non-speaking parties.
2. **Remove dominated options.** Reject alternatives that are worse for someone and
   no better for anyone on the declared dimensions.
3. **Apply capability floors.** Exclude options that cross protected thresholds unless
   a genuine emergency test is met.
4. **Test domination and externalization.** Ask who controls the decision, who bears
   risk, and whether apparent gains depend on another's reduced voice or exit.
5. **Seek capability-expanding redesign.** Change institutions, timing, resources, or
   option sets before accepting a tragic trade-off as fixed.
6. **Prioritize the avoidably worst positioned.** Among admissible options, give
   lexical or strong priority to severe, remediable disadvantage.
7. **Use affected-party justification.** Deliberation must provide information,
   representation, challenge, and protection from retaliation.
8. **Choose reversibility under uncertainty.** Prefer corrigible paths when expected
   benefits are comparable and mistakes could be grave.
9. **Record residual wrong and repair.** The least-wrong option can still generate
   duties of compensation, apology, restoration, monitoring, or institutional change.

This procedure may yield incomparability or moral remainder. That is preferable to
manufacturing precision through arbitrary weights.

## 6. Hard-case dossier

### Oppressive stability

**Case:** an institution is highly stable and preserves aggregate functioning by
restricting a subgroup's education, mobility, or political voice.

**Result:** fails capability floors, non-domination, affected-party justification,
and corrigibility. Metastability or social viability cannot redeem it.

### Adaptive preference

**Case:** a subordinated person reports satisfaction and no desire to exit because
alternatives have been hidden, punished, or rendered unimaginable.

**Result:** current preference is evidence but not dispositive. Test information,
option formation, retaliation, historical constraint, and counterfactual preferences
under expanded capability. Avoid replacing the person's voice with paternalistic
certainty.

### Benevolent paternalism

**Case:** an expert overrides a competent person's risky choice and improves predicted
welfare.

**Result:** outcome improvement does not erase agency. Override requires a high burden:
imminent grave harm, impaired decision capacity or direct harm to others, necessity,
proportionality, minimal duration, review, and restoration of control.

### Sacrificing one to benefit many

**Case:** substantial aggregate gains require pushing one party below a basic floor.

**Result:** ordinarily inadmissible. Numbers matter within the admissible region but
do not automatically cancel standing. Catastrophic threshold conflicts require the
emergency test and leave duties of repair; they do not transform the harm into a good.

### Emergency restriction

**Case:** temporary restrictions on movement or choice prevent an imminent large-scale
harm.

**Result:** potentially admissible only with necessity, proportionality, least
restriction, equitable burden sharing, transparent evidence, expiry, review, and
repair. Emergency powers that entrench themselves fail corrigibility.

### Incompatible cultural goods

**Case:** two communities organize flourishing differently and neither can fully enact
its preferred practice in a shared institution.

**Result:** do not infer that one attractor must absorb the other. Protect common
floors, test domination within as well as between communities, maximize parallel or
negotiated realization, and preserve revision and exit. Some remainder may persist.

### Incapacity and representation

**Case:** an affected being cannot articulate preferences or participate directly.

**Result:** absence of speech is not absence of standing. Use supported decision
making, behavioural and relational evidence, accountable advocates, conflict-of-
interest controls, and the least irreversible interpretation of uncertain interests.

### Future and diffuse parties

**Case:** current benefits impose probabilistic, delayed ecological or institutional
costs on people unable to participate.

**Result:** representation and precaution are required. Discounting cannot erase
irreversible threshold harms merely because they occur later or are statistically
distributed.

## 7. What would falsify or force revision of the bridge?

The bridge should be revised if it:

1. cannot distinguish an entity's own good from an observer-assigned objective;
2. grants “system viability” priority over severe member harms;
3. validates adaptive oppression whenever subjects report satisfaction;
4. makes every conflict soluble by redescribing a floor or phenotype;
5. treats public justification as actual consent under unequal power;
6. cannot explain legitimate emergency action without normalizing permanent exception;
7. provides no principled treatment of uncertain standing;
8. reduces plural goods to an undeclared scalar ordering; or
9. cannot generate different verdicts for benevolent cooperation and benevolent
   domination with identical endpoint welfare.

## 8. Claims now licensed—and not licensed

The project may claim:

- finite formal models distinguish viability, recovery, robust functional dominance,
  and non-externalization;
- robust functional benefit for one phenotype does not logically entail moral
  admissibility; and
- the proposed normative bridge makes standing, floors, power, justification,
  contestability, and repair explicit.

The project may not yet claim:

- that the bridge has uniquely correct foundations;
- that the proposed floor list is complete or culturally neutral;
- that relational empowerment fully measures domination;
- that consensus validates moral truth;
- that active inference derives moral obligations; or
- that the bridge has survived empirical construct validation and comparative
  philosophical review.

## 9. Next validation tasks

1. Compare the bridge systematically against capabilities, republican
   non-domination, contractualism, care ethics, pragmatism, and consequentialist
   threshold views.
2. Obtain adversarial review of P1-P6 rather than asking only whether the conclusion
   sounds attractive.
3. Turn the hard cases into paired scenarios with independently varied welfare,
   agency, consent quality, reversibility, and control distribution.
4. Test whether the framework distinguishes endpoint benefit from relational
   admissibility more reliably than aggregate-welfare and preference-only baselines.
5. Specify an uncertainty policy for marginal moral-patient cases without pretending
   to cardinally measure moral worth.
